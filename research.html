Research

Research statement

I aim to functionalize emergent phenomena in active matter by combining elasticity, activity and memory, enabling materials that sense, act and adapt through experience.

My work explores how non-reciprocal interactions generate nonlinear waves and collective motion for mechanical signalling and adaptive locomotion. Multistable motion patterns could provide memory, allowing past interactions to shape future responses.

At the intersection of soft matter, robotics and dynamical systems, I explore how engineering complex material behaviour can reveal the physical principles underlying lifelike functionality.

Collective motion & time crystals

How does a material find a common rhythm? We study active elastic systems in which vibrations grow in wavelength, period and amplitude until a synchronized state emerges. These experiments connect nonlinear waves, collective motion and time crystallization.

Breathing solitons

Adaptive locomotion

We build active solids that roll, crawl and adapt to uneven terrain. Their motion emerges from interactions between motorized building blocks and the environment. Elastic deformations provide feedback, allowing the material to move without a central controller.

Topological solitons

A chain of motorized rods supports self-driven waves that move in one direction. Nonreciprocal interactions make both solitons and antisolitons travel the same way, allowing repeated motion through the chain without resetting it.
